Section 01

The Core Positioning Shift

Lock this in before every call, every proposal, every follow-up.

GRAVITY Rule: Every word in your pitch that doesn't connect to time saved, money recovered, or waste eliminated is a word costing you the deal. Stop leading with AI. Lead with the problem.
PILLAR 01
Lead with Process, Not Tools
Anchor your message to bottleneck mapping and operational design. This signals you understand how the business works — not just how the software works.
PILLAR 02
Be the Architect, Not the Installer
Anyone can connect two apps. Very few can analyze a full operation and design a system that scales. Position as the architect who owns the vision.
PILLAR 03
Sell Leverage, Not AI
Companies don't want more tools — they want less friction. Lead with what disappears from their plate, not what gets added to their tech stack.
PILLAR 04
Be the ROI Partner, Not the Vendor
Vendors sell outputs. Partners sell outcomes: saved hours, headcount redirected, and revenue accelerated. Outcomes are always measured in their language.
PILLAR 05
Be the End-to-End System Builder
The person who maps, designs, builds, deploys, trains, documents, and supports eliminates nearly all competition immediately. Own the full lifecycle.

Section 03

30-Minute Discovery Call Timing Guide

The deal is won or lost here — not in the proposal, not in the follow-up.

0–2 MIN
Open & Frame
The Consultant
Set the frame. Make it about them. Use the Opening Script. No slides. No tools talk. Establish this is a diagnosis, not a demo.
2–7 MIN
Business Overview
The Architect Listening
Ask 3–5 overview questions. Let them talk 60+ seconds uninterrupted. Build your mental map: revenue, team size, volume, tech stack.
7–20 MIN
Workflow Deep Dive
The Surgeon Diagnosing
Run the 9 Deep-Dive questions. Listen for the 5 Billable System Patterns. When they describe a problem — don't solve it. Say "Tell me more about that."
20–25 MIN
Quantify ROI
The CFO Doing Math
Pull numbers FROM them using the ROI formulas. Do the math out loud together. When they confirm the number, they've sold themselves.
25–30 MIN
Close & Next Steps
The Partner
Summarize 2–3 highest-leverage opportunities. Anchor cost of inaction. Present the next step as a natural move, not a pitch.
Timing Rule: If you're still on Overview questions at the 10-minute mark, you're pitching — not diagnosing. Redirect: "Let me ask you something more specific about how your team handles [X]."
Section 04

Opening Scripts & Frame-Setting Templates

Use word-for-word or as a close guide. The first 60 seconds determine whether this is a pitch or a diagnosis.

Primary — Cold / Warm Intro
"Great to meet you. Before anything else — I want to understand how your operation actually runs day-to-day. Where your team spends time, where things get stuck, what your current tech stack looks like. From there we can identify where the biggest opportunities are. And if there's a clear fit, I'll walk you through exactly what next steps look like. Sound good?"
Referral / Warm Intro Variant
"[Name] speaks highly of what you've built. I always like to start these conversations by getting a real picture of how the operation runs — not the polished version, the real one. The busy parts, the frustrating parts, the stuff that slows everything down. That's where the value always lives. Mind if we start there?"
Inbound / Request Variant
"Thanks for reaching out. To make sure anything I put together is actually built around your business — not a generic deck — I'd love to spend most of this call in your world. Walk me through how a typical week runs for you and your team. I'll ask some pointed questions along the way."
Frame Rules
🚫 Never open with your services, tools, or case studies
🚫 Never use the word "AI" in the first 5 minutes
✅ The frame is always: Diagnosis, not Demo
✅ Use "Are you open to" for any soft ask
✅ Use "Know that" when establishing credibility
Section 05

Workflow Deep-Dive Question Bank

Run during minutes 7–20. Listen for the 5 Billable System Patterns below. Never solve in the moment — say "Tell me more about that."

The Opener — Always Lead With This
Q1
"Walk me through your process from start to finish — from the moment a new client or order comes in, what happens?"
Listen for: Where it starts, who touches it first, how information moves, where handoffs happen, where the prospect pauses or sighs.
Volume & Capacity
Q2
"Which teams rely most on manual work right now? What does a heavy day look like for them?"
Listen for: Bottlenecks, overwhelmed roles, recurring firefighting, tasks that expand to fill all available time.
Q3
"How many [leads / appointments / orders / reports / follow-ups] does your team process in a typical week?"
Listen for: Volume numbers — this is the multiplier in every ROI formula.
Tool & Integration
Q4
"Walk me through your current tech stack — what tools does your team use day-to-day?"
Listen for: 5+ disconnected tools, anything without native integration to anything else, tools they "love but hate."
Q5
"When information needs to move between those tools — how does that happen today?"
Listen for: Copy-paste, manual re-entry, email chains. That gap is a billable automation every single time.
Bottleneck & Friction
Q6
"Where does work tend to slow down or get stuck? What usually triggers that?"
Listen for: Approval delays, missing information, someone not responding fast enough — each is an automation target.
Q7
"Is there anything that basically stops when one specific person is out or unavailable?"
Listen for: Single-point-of-failure processes. These are the highest-urgency and easiest-to-close automation targets.
Owner Time & Strategic Capacity
Q8
"As the owner — what percentage of your week are you personally spending on operational tasks versus revenue-generating work?"
Listen for: Any number above 30% in ops is a major ROI opportunity. Owner time is always worth $100–$250/hr minimum.
Growth Readiness — The Anchor Close Setup
Q9
"If you doubled your volume tomorrow — which part of your operation would break first?"
Listen for: The constraint they already know about but haven't solved. This becomes the anchor point for the close and the headline of the proposal.
GRAVITY Discovery Rule: When the prospect describes a problem — don't say "we can automate that." Say "Tell me more about that." The deeper they go, the larger the deal becomes.
The 5 Billable System Patterns — Listen for These
PATTERN 01
Manual Multi-System Entry
Data entered into multiple systems by hand. Always indicates an integration gap with immediate, calculable ROI.
PATTERN 02
Single-Person Approval Bottleneck
Approvals held by one person. Work stops, backlogs build. Classic workflow automation opportunity.
PATTERN 03
Person-Dependent Process
Work stops when a specific person is unavailable. Highest urgency trigger. Easiest close because the pain is constant.
PATTERN 04
Copy-Paste Between Non-Integrated Tools
Annual cost always larger than the owner realizes. ROI calculation is immediate and undeniable — show it side by side.
PATTERN 05
Critical Info in Email / Memory
Invisible operational debt. AI knowledge base or CRM automation is the solution. Quantify with error cost formula.
Section 06

ROI Calculation Formulas & Live Scripts

All inputs come FROM the prospect — not from you. Do the math out loud. When they confirm the number, they've sold themselves.

"If I'm hearing this right — [X hours] per week across [Y people] at roughly [Z per hour], fully loaded — that's about $[Total] per year just on that one task. Does that sound right?"

Formula 01
Manual Hours Removed
hrs/wk on task × people who touch it
× fully loaded hourly rate × 52
Front desk 18 hrs/wk × $28/hr = ~$26K/yr on appointment reminders alone
Formula 02
Cycle Time Reduction
% more leads converting (speed advantage)
× avg deal value × monthly lead volume
Same-day vs. 3-day estimates: 30% more conversions × $2,500 avg = +$10K/mo
Formula 03
Error Reduction
errors per month × cost per error × 12
5 errors/mo at $500 each = $30K/yr in rework, refunds, and lost trust
Formula 04
Team Capacity Unlocked
owner/manager hrs/wk on ops tasks
× conservative hourly value × 52
10 hrs/wk × $150/hr = $78K/yr in redirectable strategic capacity
Formula 05
Headcount Cost Displaced
fully loaded new hire cost
vs. monthly AI agent cost × 12
$60K hire vs. $500/mo AI receptionist = $54K/yr savings at 80% task coverage
GRAVITY ROI Rule: Never estimate the number for them. The inputs must come from their mouth. Their number. Their confirmation. That is your proposal's anchor — and you can quote them directly back to them in writing.
Section 07

Objection Response Scripts

The three most common local business objections — and why each is actually a buying signal when you know how to flip it.

⏱
"I don't have time to implement something new."
Why It's Good News
They're busy BECAUSE of the exact work this system eliminates. This is a threshold problem, not a timing problem. Budget is confirmed. Urgency is confirmed. They just don't see the exit yet.
GRAVITY Flip
Agree with the symptom, not the conclusion. Make the ask feel small. Frame it as removing from their plate — not adding to it.
"You don't have time because of the exact work this system removes. We're not adding to your plate — we're deploying AI that takes it off. Three hours of your time over two weeks. We handle everything else. It works on day one."
💡
"I've tried something like this before and it didn't work."
Why It's Good News
They have budget. They understand the concept. They know what failure looks like. This is the most educated, solution-aware prospect you can find. They're not saying no — they're saying "convince me you're different."
GRAVITY Flip
Let them describe what went wrong. 9 times out of 10 someone built one piece without understanding the full system. Their bad experience becomes the argument for your process.
"What happened? [Listen.] Nine times out of ten, someone built one piece without understanding how everything connects. That's exactly why we map the full operation first. Your experience becomes the argument for why our process is different — and why this time the system actually holds."
🧠
"My business is different — you can't automate what we do."
Why It's Good News
They're protecting their identity and expertise, not rejecting automation. They believe their judgment and relationships are irreplaceable — which is correct. Agree with that truth, then show what sits below it.
GRAVITY Flip
Agree first. Never say "we can automate everything." Show what the AI employees handle — the administrative layer — so their team focuses 100% on human work.
"You're right — the judgment and relationship parts, we never touch those. What we handle: inquiries answered 24/7, reviews generated automatically, CRM updated after every interaction. What's left is your team spending 100% of their time on work only humans can do."
GRAVITY Objection Rule: Never defend. Never pitch. Agree with the truth in their objection — then redirect to the cost of the problem they just described.
